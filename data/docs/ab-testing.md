# A/B Testing

A/B testing compares two variants (control and treatment) on a metric to decide whether a change works. You define a hypothesis, choose a primary metric (the thing you want to move), secondary metrics, and guardrail metrics (metrics you must not harm). The minimum detectable effect (MDE) is the smallest effect worth detecting; it sets the sample size together with baseline conversion and statistical power (typically 80%). After assignment, you check that groups are balanced. You run a statistical test: a z-test or t-test for proportions and means, with multiple-comparison correction when several metrics are tested. Significance is judged by the p-value against a pre-set alpha (usually 0.05), but the decision also considers effect size, confidence intervals, and guardrail health. A test without a pre-registered hypothesis and a fixed sample size is not a valid experiment.
